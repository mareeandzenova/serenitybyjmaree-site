The Failed Yogi

on losing the path and seeing yourself clearly for the first time

I thought I was on the path to enlightenment. I had the practice, the teachers, the Sanskrit. I had the quiet mornings and the willingness to sit with discomfort. I had, I was certain, arrived somewhere close to the beginning of something real.

But somewhere along the way, I got lost. And what I didn't realise — not for years — was that losing the path might have been the most honest thing I ever did.

The moment I stopped performing the version of myself I'd been trained to become, I started to feel something I'd almost forgotten was possible. My own weight, on my own feet.

There's a particular kind of failure that happens quietly. Not the dramatic exit — the public unravelling, the visible collapse. This is smaller. It's the slow withdrawal from a practice that no longer fits the body wearing it. It's the morning you roll out the mat and feel nothing, and keep rolling it out anyway, because stopping would mean admitting something you don't yet have language for.

What I was protecting

I think what I was protecting, in all those years of dedicated practice, was a version of myself I'd built the rest of my life around. To step off the path was to step into the question: if not this, then what? Who is the person who has been practising for twenty-five years — and what does she do when the practice stops feeling like hers?

I didn't have an answer. I still don't, not entirely. But I've stopped expecting the answer to arrive from the outside — from a teacher, a lineage, a workshop. It comes, when it comes, from the body. And the body required me to stop performing long enough to hear it.

I'm writing this not because I've resolved anything. I'm writing it because I know some of you are standing in the same place I was standing — somewhere between who you were trained to be and who you actually are. And I wanted you to know: losing the path is not the opposite of the practice. For some of us, it's where the practice finally begins.
