Take the Chance. You're Grown Now.

On inherited belief systems, the gap between who you were performing and who you actually are.

There was a version of my life that made complete sense on paper. The kind of life that looks responsible from the outside.

Measured.
Considered.

Built around the core values of a single black woman raising a child while working in corporate along lines that were laid down long before I was old enough to question them.

I followed those lines for a long time until I no longer recognized myself inside them. They were woven in early — ideas about what a good life looks like, what a woman should want, what it means to be practical, to be safe, to be grateful for what you have. And in questioning them, I began to find out who I actually was.

I had never actually chosen any of it and what I was beginning to choose didn't make sense to those around me.

What I didn't know was that my inherited belief system was still running in the background. That's the thing about inherited belief systems. They don't announce themselves. They just quietly run, shaping every decision, every hesitation, every moment you talked yourself out of something that felt alive to you. You don't question them because they feel like you. They're so close you can't see the edges.

But at some point — and I couldn't tell you exactly when — I started to feel the gap between who I was performing and who I actually was.

I started asking different questions. Not the ones I was raised with, but my own. What do I actually value? What kind of life feels true to me, not just acceptable? What have I been carrying that was never really mine to carry?

Untangling that is slow, uncomfortable work. Some of the beliefs I let go of belonged to people I love. That part isn't clean. There's grief in it. But there's also something on the other side of it — a kind of groundedness I didn't have access to when I was still living inside someone else's framework.

Which is what keeps bringing me back to this:

Take the chance. Because you're grown.

I'm not writing this because I figured it out. The path isn't clear. The fear isn't gone. I still struggle to know the difference between caution and avoidance. But I know the difference between a life that fits and a life that was simply handed to you and never returned. At some point, life stops being about how you were raised and starts being about how you decide to live.

I'm writing it because I'm still in it — and something about saying that out loud feels important right now.

Here are some things I've been sitting with, if you want to go there too:

What beliefs am I still carrying that I never actually chose?

Where have I been mistaking someone else's values for my own?

What would I take a chance on, if I trusted that I was allowed to?
